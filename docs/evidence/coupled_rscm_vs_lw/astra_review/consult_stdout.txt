## Recommended fix

**Correct the report without discarding the PCA results.** Suggested verdict: “CRSCM loses at 16 PCs; at 64 PCs it improves rare-class averages and FIN-Benthic2’s primary macro outcome, while micro outcomes show no clear gain. Results do not establish a universal winner.”

**Native-dimension assessment — recommendation:** yes, 1024 dimensions would be more informative about the original frozen representation and low-sample covariance estimation. It is not required to make the approved PCA experiment valid. LW explicitly includes fixed-dimensional asymptotics; neither estimator requires \(p>n\). See Ledoit–Wolf §3.1, Assumption 1, [local text:393](/home/aijyu/Documents/Research/methods/ledoit_wolf2004/paper.txt:393).

What changes:

- Most class SCMs become singular. LW’s positive identity term can restore definiteness; CRSCM can obtain support through pooling and/or identity shrinkage. A pooled matrix is not automatically full rank, and CRSCM permits zero identity shrinkage.
- Gaussian likelihood weights residual errors by inverse covariance. Small estimated eigenvalues, accumulated tail-direction errors and class-mean estimation error can dominate. Frobenius-MSE tuning emphasizes a different loss.
- Pooled train PCA retains high *total* variance, including between-class variation. It discards directions that may matter for within-class density, particularly for rare classes. It also changes both identity targets and estimated shrinkage parameters.
- Larger dimension does not guarantee reliable plug-ins at \(n=15\). CRSCM §IV-B gives a sphericity condition, \(\gamma/p\to0\), for an asymptotic result with known location; §VI-A reports bias for compound-symmetry spectra. Estimated spatial medians, correlated coordinates and non-elliptical classes add uncertainty. Increasing \(p\) does not supply independent specimens.

Consequently, **either direction of reversal is plausible**: pooling may estimate useful missing directions, or import inappropriate tail covariance and worsen conditioning.

A minimal predeclared follow-up:

1. Use unchanged caches, eligible classes, means, five folds and both datasets; native coordinates, no PCA/whitening or validation tuning. Keep macro primary, micro secondary and the existing count strata; additionally report \(n_c/p\) and \(n_c\le p\).
2. Compare CRSCM−LW **within the same dimension**. Do not interpret absolute likelihoods—or gain magnitudes across dimensions—as equivalent quantities. Do not implement a validation-derived hybrid rule.
3. Report per-fold/class differences, conditional paired intervals, counts, parameters, plug-in estimates and clipping frequencies, minimum eigenvalues, conditioning, failures, runtime and memory. Separate log-determinant and Mahalanobis contributions.
4. Use float64 and Cholesky scoring; check finite inputs, parameters and scores, symmetry, constant coordinates and pooled rank. Preserve failed classes in accounting; label any successful-fit-only aggregate. No silent jitter or pseudoinverse density.
5. Audit convergence: the authors’ stopping expression permits cancellation between parameter changes, and its iteration-limit warning is unreachable ([authors’ code:471](/home/aijyu/Documents/Feature-Geometry/third_party/coupled_rscm/multiclass_rscm.py:471)).
